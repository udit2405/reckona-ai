Employer branding in the AI era: what candidates actually check

By Reckona AIUpdated 26 July 20267 min read

Before a strong candidate applies, they increasingly ask ChatGPT what it's like to work at your company — not just Glassdoor. If the answer is vague, outdated, or wrong, you lose candidates you never even see apply. Here's what to fix.

The new pre-application research

Candidates used to check a careers page and maybe Glassdoor. Now many also ask an AI assistant directly — "what's it like working at [company]," "does [company] offer remote work," "what's the culture like." If your information online is thin or contradictory, the AI answer reflects that uncertainty, and a strong candidate moves on to a company with a clearer signal.

What to get right

SignalFix
Careers page factsCurrent roles, real perks, accurate remote/hybrid policy — kept up to date
Consistency across platformsLinkedIn, Glassdoor and your site should agree, not contradict each other
Employee voiceGenuine posts from your team read as more credible than polished corporate copy alone
Specificity"Remote-first, Mumbai hub, quarterly offsites" beats "great culture, fun team"

Why specificity matters more than polish

Vague claims give AI engines nothing concrete to repeat accurately, so they either omit you or hedge. Specific, verifiable facts — actual perks, actual policies, actual team size — are exactly the kind of content AI engines can confidently quote, which is also exactly what candidates trust more than a generic pitch.

Treat your careers page like a product page: specific, current, and worth citing accurately — not a static block of text nobody has touched in a year.

Where this connects

This is the same entity-clarity discipline behind Entity SEO — being a legible, accurately described entity helps AI engines get your employer brand right, not just your product.
