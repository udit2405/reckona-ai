Invoice processing automation: what accuracy to actually expect

By Reckona AIUpdated 26 July 20268 min read

Vendors promise "99% accurate" invoice automation, and then it breaks on the first handwritten note or unusual format. Here's a realistic picture of what accuracy to expect, and — more importantly — how good systems handle the cases that don't hit it.

Accuracy is a range, not a single number

Invoice typeRealistic extraction accuracy
Standard, consistent vendor templates95%+ straightforward
Mixed formats across many vendors85-92%, more exceptions
Scanned, handwritten or poor-qualityMeaningfully lower — plan for review

A vendor quoting a single flat "99% accurate" number without specifying which invoice type they mean is a claim worth being skeptical of.

Where accuracy actually comes from

  • PO matching. Cross-checking extracted data against a purchase order catches errors extraction alone would miss.
  • Vendor-specific templates. Systems that learn each vendor's format outperform generic OCR by a wide margin.
  • Confidence scoring. The system should flag its own uncertainty — a low-confidence extraction routed to a human beats a wrong number processed silently.

Designing for the exceptions

The real design question isn't "how do we hit 99%" — it's "what happens to the invoices that don't." Good systems route low-confidence extractions to a human reviewer automatically, log the correction, and use it to improve future matching. That handoff is what separates automation that survives real operations from a demo that breaks in production.

Sequencing rule: start with your highest-volume, most consistent vendor format first. Prove the system there before expanding to messier formats — see the broader ranking in document processing automation.
