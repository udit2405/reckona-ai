How to reduce cart abandonment: a practical checklist

By Reckona AIUpdated 29 July 20267 min read

Cart abandonment rates commonly sit above 60-70% across ecommerce, which sounds alarming until you realize it's mostly normal browsing behavior — the real opportunity is the meaningful share that's recoverable with the right fixes.

Checkout friction worth removing first

Friction pointFix
Forced account creationOffer guest checkout — this alone recovers a meaningful share of abandonments
Unexpected costs at final stepShow shipping and taxes earlier in the flow, not as a checkout-page surprise
Too many form fieldsOnly ask what's needed for this transaction — address autocomplete helps too
Limited payment optionsUPI, cards, wallets, COD where relevant — missing a preferred method loses the sale

Recovery flows worth automating

  • Abandoned cart email/WhatsApp sequence — first message within an hour, a reminder at 24 hours, a final nudge (sometimes with an incentive) at 48-72 hours.
  • Exit-intent offers for on-site abandonment signals, used sparingly to avoid training customers to always wait for a discount.
  • Retargeting ads for cart abandoners specifically, not just general site visitors.

Realistic recovery rate expectations

A well-built recovery sequence typically recovers a meaningful minority of abandoned carts — not a majority. Treat it as reclaiming otherwise-lost revenue at near-zero marginal cost, not as a fix that eliminates abandonment. The bigger lever is usually removing the friction that caused the abandonment in the first place.

Don't over-rely on discount-based recovery. Constant discount nudges in recovery emails train customers to abandon deliberately, expecting a coupon — reserve deeper discounts for genuinely price-sensitive segments, not every abandoner.
