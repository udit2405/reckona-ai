E-E-A-T for Indian websites: what Google (and AI) actually check

By Reckona AIUpdated 27 July 20268 min read

E-E-A-T — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust — gets treated as an abstract Google buzzword. It isn't. It's a checklist of concrete, buildable signals, and increasingly the same signals AI engines use to decide whether to trust and cite you.

The four signals, made concrete

SignalWhat actually demonstrates it
ExperienceFirst-hand detail a competitor copying your content couldn't fake — real photos, specific numbers, an actual process described
ExpertiseAuthor credentials visible, content that goes beyond surface-level advice
AuthoritativenessOther sites and sources citing or linking to you as a reference
TrustAccurate contact information, clear business identity, no misleading claims

Why this matters more for YMYL-adjacent categories

Healthcare, finance, and legal content — "Your Money or Your Life" categories — face the highest E-E-A-T bar, because inaccurate advice there causes real harm. If you're in healthcare or BFSI, this isn't optional polish — it's the difference between ranking at all and being suppressed.

Concrete ways to build it

  • Author bios with real credentials on expert content — not "Admin" or no byline at all.
  • An About page that actually says who you are — team, history, physical address — not a generic paragraph.
  • Original data and first-hand case studies instead of rewritten generic advice available on a hundred other sites.
  • Third-party validation — press mentions, industry directory listings, genuine reviews.

E-E-A-T and GEO are converging: the same trust signals that satisfy Google's quality raters are what AI engines weigh when deciding whether to cite you confidently. Building one builds the other.
