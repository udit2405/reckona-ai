Cheapest SEO price in India: what ₹2,999–5,000/month actually gets you

By Reckona AIUpdated 2 August 20267 min read

Search "cheapest SEO price in India" and you'll find packages from ₹2,000 to ₹8,000 a month. They're real, they're not scams in the legal sense, and they can rank low-competition local terms. Here's exactly what's inside one, why it's that cheap, and when it quietly costs more than a proper engagement.

The actual price floor

Rock-bottom₹2,000 – ₹4,999Freelancers on Fiverr/local classifieds, one-person "agencies"
Budget agency₹5,000 – ₹8,000Small local agencies running the same 5 tactics for every client

Below ₹15,000/month, you're not buying a smaller version of proper SEO — you're buying a different, much narrower product. Knowing exactly what that product is stops you from being disappointed by something that was never going to include what you assumed.

What's actually inside a ₹2,999–5,000 package

  • A templated monthly report — usually a rank-tracker screenshot and a keyword count, not analysis.
  • Generic on-page tweaks — meta titles and alt text edited once, rarely revisited.
  • Bulk, low-quality backlinks — directory submissions, comment spam, or PBN links bought in bulk. This is the single biggest risk in the package (more below).
  • Zero or AI-only content — one unedited AI-generated article a month, if content is included at all.
  • No technical work — Core Web Vitals, crawl errors and schema are rarely touched; they take real time per site, which doesn't fit the price.
  • One person running 30–50 client accounts — the math of the price only works at that volume, which is why nothing is customised to your business.

Why it's priced that low — the honest reason

It's not charity and it's not always a scam — it's volume. One person managing 40 accounts at ₹4,000 each is ₹1.6L/month, which is a viable income if each account takes under an hour a month. The only way to hit that is templated, non-custom, mostly automated work. The price is a direct, honest signal of the time being spent on your account: roughly 45–60 minutes a month, total.

The one question that tells you everything: ask what happens differently for your business specifically versus every other client on the same package. If the honest answer is "nothing," you now know exactly what you're buying — a templated service, not a strategy.

The real cost when it goes wrong

The failure mode isn't usually "nothing happens" — it's active damage that costs more to undo than a proper engagement would have cost in the first place:

  • A Google manual action from spam links. Recovery means a disavow file, a reconsideration request, and 2–6 months of near-zero visibility while Google re-evaluates — losing far more than the ₹40,000–₹1L saved over that period.
  • Duplicate or thin AI content indexed at scale. Once Google associates a domain with low-quality content, recovering trust takes months of consistently better content — you pay twice for the same pages.
  • Months burned with no read on whether SEO works at all. The most expensive outcome isn't money — it's a founder concluding "SEO doesn't work for us" after 6 months of a package that was never capable of moving the needle, and writing off the channel entirely.

When the cheapest option is actually fine

Not every business should avoid this tier — it's a reasonable choice when:

  • You're pre-revenue or pre-launch and the real goal is just claiming your Google Business Profile and basic on-page hygiene, not competing for traffic yet.
  • Your category has near-zero organic search competition, so even generic tactics can rank you.
  • You treat it explicitly as a 3–6 month placeholder before a real budget, not a long-term strategy.

It stops being fine the moment you're relying on it to actually grow revenue against real competition, or you don't know what's happening to your backlink profile in the background.

A 2-minute check before you buy

  1. Ask for 3 examples of link-building sources they'll use for your specific site. "We build quality backlinks" with no examples is the biggest red flag.
  2. Ask who else in your city or category they currently manage. If it's a direct competitor, your "strategy" is being split with theirs.
  3. Ask what a manual Google penalty would mean for your contract. A provider confident in their tactics will answer this without hesitation.
