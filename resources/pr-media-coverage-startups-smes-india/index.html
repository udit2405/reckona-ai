PR and media coverage for startups and SMEs in India

By Reckona AIUpdated 29 July 20268 min read

Most small businesses assume press coverage is reserved for funded startups with PR agencies on retainer. It isn't — genuine, newsworthy stories get covered regardless of company size, if pitched correctly.

What actually makes a story newsworthy

AngleWhy journalists respond to it
Original data or researchGives the journalist something exclusive and citable — our own brand visibility study is exactly this format
A genuine trend you're seeing earlyJournalists want to be first to a real shift, not a rehash
A specific, quantified result"We cut X by Y%" beats "we're growing fast" every time
Expert commentary on breaking newsTimely reaction to something already happening, offering a genuine expert angle

How to actually pitch a journalist

  • Research who covers your beat specifically — a generic mass email to fifty journalists reads as spam and gets ignored.
  • Lead with the story, not your company. "Company X launches" is rarely news; "here's a surprising trend we found" often is.
  • Keep the pitch short. A journalist decides in seconds whether to read further — three tight paragraphs beat a five-page press release.
  • Make yourself easy to quote. Offer a specific, quotable line, not vague corporate-speak.

Why PR pays off beyond backlinks

A genuine press mention builds third-party credibility no amount of self-published content can replicate — it's also exactly the kind of cross-source corroboration that both traditional SEO authority and AI engines evaluating your entity weight heavily. One real feature often does more for trust than months of owned-channel content.

Start with one genuinely good story, not a press blast. A single well-pitched, newsworthy angle to the right five journalists beats a generic release sent to five hundred.
