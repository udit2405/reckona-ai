Does AI-generated content hurt your SEO rankings?

By Reckona AIUpdated 27 July 20268 min read

Google has said repeatedly that it doesn't penalize content for being AI-assisted — it penalizes content that's low-quality, regardless of how it was made. The distinction matters more than most teams treat it, and getting it wrong is an easy way to waste a lot of publishing volume.

What Google actually penalizes

Google's public guidance focuses on quality signals, not authorship method: original insight, accuracy, usefulness to the reader and demonstrated expertise. Content produced purely to manipulate rankings — thin, unoriginal, mass-published without editorial review — is what gets targeted, whether a human or an AI wrote the first draft.

Where teams actually get burned

Risky patternWhy it's risky
Publishing AI drafts unedited, at high volumeGeneric, interchangeable content with no genuine expertise or original insight
No fact-checking passAI models produce plausible-sounding errors — see our content audit checklist
Same prompt output across many pagesNear-duplicate content across a site dilutes relevance signals

How to use AI in your content workflow safely

  • AI drafts, a human edits. Use AI to accelerate the first pass, not to skip editorial judgment.
  • Add something AI can't generate. Original data, a specific client example, a genuinely contrarian opinion — the parts that make content worth citing.
  • Fact-check before publishing. Especially numbers, claims and anything that could become an AI hallucination about your own brand if wrong.

The real question isn't "AI or human" — it's "does this page genuinely help the reader more than what's already ranking." Answer that honestly before publishing, regardless of how the draft was produced.
