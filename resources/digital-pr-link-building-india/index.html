Digital PR and link building for Indian businesses: what works in 2026

By Reckona AIUpdated 27 July 20268 min read

Backlinks still matter for rankings, but the tactics that worked five years ago — mass guest posting, directory submissions, link exchanges — mostly don't anymore. Here's what actually earns links in 2026.

What stopped working

  • Low-quality guest posting. Google has gotten much better at discounting links from obvious link farms and paid-placement networks.
  • Directory submissions at scale. A handful of relevant, high-quality directories help; hundreds of generic ones do nothing.
  • Reciprocal link exchanges. Easily detected, and increasingly ignored rather than penalized — which means wasted effort either way.

What actually earns links now

TacticWhy it works
Original research or dataJournalists and bloggers cite sources with a genuine data point, not opinion pieces
Expert commentary (HARO-style)Responding to journalist queries earns links from real publications
Genuinely useful free toolsCalculators, checkers and assessments get linked to and shared organically
Founder visibilityA quoted founder in industry press brings a link home along with credibility

Digital PR vs traditional link building

The distinction matters: traditional link building asks "how do I get a link," while digital PR asks "what would a journalist actually want to cover" and the link follows naturally. The second approach produces fewer but far higher-quality links — and the coverage itself builds brand awareness independent of the SEO value.

Prioritization rule: if you have limited SEO budget, technical foundations and content come before link building — links amplify good content, they don't substitute for it.
