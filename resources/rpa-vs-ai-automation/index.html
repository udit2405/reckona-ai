RPA vs AI automation: what's the difference and which do you need

By Reckona AIUpdated 27 July 20267 min read

RPA (Robotic Process Automation) and AI automation get used almost interchangeably, but they solve different problems. Picking the wrong one for a task means either overpaying for capability you don't need, or building something that breaks constantly.

The core distinction

RPAAI automation
How it worksFollows exact, pre-defined rules and clicks/stepsUses models to interpret, extract and generate — handles variation
Best forHighly structured, unchanging processesProcesses involving unstructured input — documents, language, judgment-adjacent decisions
Fails whenThe input format changes even slightlyRare, ambiguous edge cases — needs a human fallback
ExampleCopying fixed-format data from System A to System B nightlyReading varied-format vendor invoices and extracting the right fields

When RPA is actually the right choice

If a process is completely rule-based and the input never varies — a scheduled data transfer between two systems with a fixed format — RPA is simpler, cheaper and more predictable than an AI-based approach. Don't reach for AI where a simpler rule-based tool does the job just as well.

When you need AI automation instead

Anything involving reading unstructured text, handling format variation, or making judgment-adjacent calls needs AI, not RPA. Document processing, customer queries, and content generation all fall here — see our AI automation starting guide.

They're often combined: AI extracts and interprets messy input, then RPA-style rules handle the predictable downstream steps. The two aren't rivals — they're different tools in the same toolbox.
