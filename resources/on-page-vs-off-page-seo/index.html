On-page vs off-page SEO: what each actually covers

By Reckona AIUpdated 27 July 20267 min read

These two terms get thrown around as if everyone agrees on the boundary. They don't always. Here's a clean definition of each, with real examples, and how to decide which deserves attention first.

The clean split

On-page SEOOff-page SEO
Anything you control directly on your own siteAnything that happens elsewhere on the web that affects your rankings
Title tags, content, internal links, schema, page speedBacklinks, brand mentions, reviews, social signals
You can fix it todayYou can influence it, but not fully control it

On-page: what to actually check

  • Title tags and meta descriptions unique per page
  • Content that directly answers the search intent, not just mentions the keyword
  • Technical health — see our technical SEO checklist
  • Internal linking connecting related pages — see our internal linking guide

Off-page: what to actually build

  • Backlinks from relevant, credible sites — see digital PR and link building
  • Genuine reviews and reputation signals
  • Brand mentions, even unlinked ones, which still build entity recognition

Prioritization rule: if your on-page fundamentals are weak, fix those first — off-page effort amplifies good content, it doesn't rescue bad content. Most sites underinvest in on-page basics before chasing links.
